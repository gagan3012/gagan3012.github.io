I am a first-year PhD candidate at the Chair of Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence (CSAI), University of Technology Nuremberg, advised by Steffen Eger. My research centers on how large language models evaluate generated text and reason about time: building deterministic and multimodal metrics for generative-text evaluation, and studying how models represent temporal and linguistic change.

Before starting my PhD, I completed an MSc in Artificial Intelligence at the University of Aberdeen (2024) and a BASc at the University of British Columbia (2022). During and after my undergraduate studies, I worked with Muhammad Abdul-Mageed's Deep Learning & Natural Language Processing Group at UBC, contributing to Peacock, Dallah, Qalam, and FinTral — multimodal Arabic and financial large language models published at ACL and ArabicNLP.

My current work spans multilingual generative-text evaluation, temporal reasoning and tokenisation in LLMs, and low-resource and Arabic NLP. My papers have appeared at ACL, EMNLP, NAACL, and ArabicNLP, and I collaborate with research groups across evaluation, temporal reasoning, and multilingual NLP.
